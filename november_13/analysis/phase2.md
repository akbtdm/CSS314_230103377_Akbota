**Serial fraction and Amdahl ceiling.** At p = 1 Stage A takes 15.2 ms of the 1090.58 ms trial, so the measured serial fraction is s = 0.0139. Amdahl's law then predicts S∞ = 1/s = 71.8 and S(P_log = 10) = 1 / (0.0139 + 0.9861/10) = 8.89 (E₁₀ = 0.89). The observed strong-scaling speedup at p = 10 is 4.58 (E₁₀ = 0.46), i.e. only 52 % of the Amdahl prediction. Amdahl's bound is not violated; it is simply far away, because its only assumption about the hardware is "p identical workers", and the M4 is not ten identical cores.

**Performance/efficiency-core inflection.** The CPU has no SMT (P_phys = P_log = 10), so the boundary the lab asks about is the one between the 4 performance cores and the 6 efficiency cores. The marginal speedup per added worker (ΔS = S_p − S_{p−1}) makes it obvious:

| added worker p | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| marginal ΔS | 0.923 | 0.789 | 0.557 | 0.186 | 0.296 | 0.185 | 0.286 | 0.245 | 0.118 |

Across the boundary p = 4 → 5 the marginal speedup drops from 0.557 to 0.186, a **−66.6 %** change for one added worker. Averaged over the two regimes, workers 2–4 add 0.756 of a core each, workers 5–10 add 0.219 each (−71 %). Expressed as capacity: going from 4 to 10 workers only shortens the trial from 333.69 ms to 237.92 ms (×1.40), so the six efficiency cores together contribute about 1.6 performance-core equivalents for this kernel, ≈0.27 of a P-core each (this includes any clock reduction the P-cores suffer when all ten cores are busy). Parallel efficiency falls from 0.82 at p = 4 to 0.69 at p = 5 and 0.46 at p = 10. The 4-worker point is also where the Amdahl curve and the measurement start to diverge sharply (3.84 predicted vs 3.27 observed; the gap is only 0.05 at p = 2).

The per-worker telemetry of Phase 3A shows the same thing from another angle: with ten barrier-synchronised FMA workers every process achieved 0.12–0.15 GFLOP/s, with no bimodal split into "fast" and "slow" workers, so macOS time-slices and migrates the processes across both clusters rather than pinning six of them to efficiency cores. Static equal partitioning therefore does not produce a single visible straggler, but the aggregate throughput of the machine still grows by only ≈0.2 core-equivalents per added worker beyond four.

**Weak scaling (Gustafson).** With N(p) = p × 250 000 the work per worker is constant, yet the trial grows from 170.80 ms (p = 1) to 351.01 ms (p = 10): weak-scaling efficiency drops to E_weak,10 = 0.487. Two components grow: Stage A, which is serial in the parent and proportional to N(p), goes from 2.39 ms to 27.37 ms (α rises from 0.0140 to 0.0780), and Stage B itself goes from 168.19 ms to 323.64 ms although every worker processes exactly 250 000 elements, again because the fifth and later workers run on slower cores and all ten share the package power budget. The Gustafson scaled speedup computed from α alone is S_G(10) = 10 − 0.078 × 9 = 9.30, which looks almost ideal. The contrast between S_G = 9.30 and E_weak = 0.49 is the real lesson of Experiment 2B: Gustafson's formula only accounts for the serial fraction and assumes that the parallel part keeps the same per-worker time at any p, which is false on a heterogeneous, power-limited laptop CPU. Both laws describe the software; the ceiling that actually binds here is the hardware topology.
