**Ceilings.** Ten workers each copying a 6.4 MB slice between two 64 MB shared buffers: median worker copy 1.193 ms, so the lab formula gives **BW_sustained = 2 × 64 × 10⁶ / T_median = 107.26 GB/s**. The per-worker times are bimodal — four workers at ≈0.6 ms (≈21 GB/s of read+write traffic each, the performance cores) and six at ≈1.2–1.3 ms (≈10 GB/s each, the efficiency cores) — so the copies do not fully overlap and the median is an efficiency-core time. Dividing the traffic actually moved (9 reps × 10 workers × 2 × 6.4 MB = 1.15 GB) by the wall time of the whole block (13.1 ms) gives **87.9 GB/s** (87–94 GB/s over three rounds), which is the physically meaningful sustained DRAM bandwidth (the M4's LPDDR5X interface peaks at ≈120 GB/s); both numbers are reported. The compute ceiling is **P_sustained = 1.2724 GFLOP/s** for ten barrier-synchronised 16-way-unrolled FMA loops (6 × 10⁷ FLOPs each; per worker 0.127–0.152 GFLOP/s, i.e. ≈15 ns per `x = x*a + b` statement — the cost of ~5 bytecodes, not of the FP unit). Ridge point **I\* = 1.2724 / 107.26 = 0.0119 FLOP/Byte** (0.0145 with the wall-clock bandwidth): in pure Python every kernel with more than ≈0.01 FLOP per byte is compute-(interpreter-)bound.

**GEMM and BS\* vs cache sizes.** All three variants agree bit-for-bit (max|C(V1) − C(V2)| = max|C(V3) − C(V2)| = 0, same k-summation order). V1 (i-j-k) 0.2081 GFLOP/s, V2 (i-k-j) 0.4589 GFLOP/s, V3 blocked: 0.2289 (BS 8), 0.2575 (16), 0.2689 (32), 0.2729 (64), 0.2780 (96), 0.2777 (128). Nominal **BS\* = 96**, but BS = 32…128 lie within 3.4 % of each other while the IQRs are 4–9 % of the medians: the curve saturates at BS ≥ 32. Tile footprints W = 3·BS²·8 B: 1.5 KB (8), 6 KB (16), 24.6 KB (32), 98 KB (64), 221 KB (96), 393 KB (128). Capacity reasoning predicts a peak where the tile fills L1d — BS ≈ 48 for the 64 KB efficiency-core L1d, BS ≈ 64 for the 128 KB performance-core L1d — followed by a drop; the measured curve rises up to that region but never drops, and the 221 KB tile of BS\* = 96 fits only in L2 (4 MB / 16 MB), not in L1d. The inner statement `c_local[c_off + j] += a_ik * B[b_off + j]` costs ≈100 ns of interpreter time, which hides an L2 hit (and a prefetched DRAM access) completely; what the sweep measures is **loop overhead**: small tiles pay the `range()` setup, the `a_ik` load and the offset arithmetic once per BS multiply-adds, and growing BS amortises that (+21 % from BS 8 to BS 96) until it no longer matters. With N = 256 the whole B matrix is 512 KB and A + B + C = 1.57 MB sit in L2 for the entire run, so there is no DRAM traffic to tile away in the first place.

**Roofline mapping.** With I\* ≈ 0.012 FLOP/Byte every variant is on the flat compute roof: attainable = min(P_sustained, I × BW) = P_sustained = 1.2724 GFLOP/s both for I_naive ≈ 0.125 FLOP/Byte (V1, V2) and for I_tiled(96) = 96/12 = 8 FLOP/Byte (V3). Achieved: **V1 16.4 %, V2 36.1 %, V3(BS\*) 21.8 %** of attainable. Even the naive kernel's memory limit, 0.125 × 107.26 = 13.4 GFLOP/s, is 10× above the interpreter ceiling. V2 beats the best blocked variant by 1.65× and the naive one by 2.21× because it executes the fewest bytecodes per multiply-add (one index into two local lists, no offset arithmetic, no block bookkeeping); in pure Python the optimisation that works is cutting interpreter work, not cache blocking.
